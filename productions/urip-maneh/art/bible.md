# Art bible: Urip Maneh

Version 1. Every image request for this production starts with the locked text below. A change to the locked text makes version 2.

The sky never comes from a painting. The theatre's cyclorama lights it.

The style is wayang beber, the old Javanese painted scroll. One hand paints every century, so a 1990s street and a Majapahit temple belong to the same scroll.

STYLE: A grand hand-made toy theatre production built from printed card. Every piece is a flat cutout of thick card about 4 mm thick, cut by hand with a craft knife. The front of each piece is painted in the manner of wayang beber, the old Javanese painted scroll: flat opaque colour on warm bark paper, fine dark brown ink outlines of even weight, elegant elongated figures with long graceful necks, slender arms and calm almond eyes, garments and surfaces filled with small hand-drawn batik patterns of dots, scrolls and parang diagonals, and trees and clouds as stylised ornamental curls. No shading gradients and no perspective depth: everything is flat and decorative, with a little gold on ornament. Bold, clear shapes that read from the back row. Puppet joints are brass split pins. Palette: bark paper #E6D3A8, soga brown #6B3F1F, indigo #23375C, madder red #9E2B25, ochre #C8923A, leaf green #4F6B3A, gold #C9A24B, ink #2A1A10, with raw card brown #A57E52 at cut edges. The mood is tender and longing, across six hundred years of Java.
CONSTRAINTS: No text, no characters, no letters, no numbers, no signs with writing, no logos, no signature, no watermark. No photographic people, no 3D render look, no plastic, no glossy finish, no pixel art.
