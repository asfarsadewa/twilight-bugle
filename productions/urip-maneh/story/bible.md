# Story bible: Urip Maneh

Two souls keep missing each other across lifetimes in Java. A kayon ends each life. In the last life, a woman stops at an old temple relief. A stone carver cut her face into that relief six hundred years before.

The song comes from the film *Urip Maneh* in the sibling repository mini-mv-studio. The show uses take A and its hand-timed lines. The theatre stages its own version with cut card, rods, strings and real light.

## Cast

| Figure | Lives |
|---|---|
| Bayu | A stone carver of Majapahit, a clerk of the 1930s, a young man of the 1990s, a man of today |
| Laras | A noblewoman of Majapahit, a passenger of the 1930s, a young woman of the 1990s, a woman of today |
| The dalang and the gamelan | They sit in front of the screen. The kayon is theirs. |

## Motifs

- **The kayon.** It crosses the stage on a rod at each change of life. In wayang, the kayon opens and closes each scene.
- **The relief.** He cuts her face in the first life. She finds it in the last life.
- **The reaching hand.** He reaches in every life. In the last life, her hand answers.

## Story by section

| Section | Time | Stage |
|---|---|---|
| Intro | 0 to 14 | The shadow screen glows. The gamelan sits in front of it. The kayon stands in the middle, then sweeps away. |
| Verse 1 | 14 to 28 | Majapahit, morning. He cuts her face on the beat. She passes behind him with her attendants. He looks up too late. |
| Verse 2 | 28 to 42 | A river port, 1930s, night. The ship leaves with her on deck. He runs to the quay and reaches out. |
| Pre-chorus | 45 to 55 | A street, 1990s. They pass in the crowd. Each stops and looks back. Each walks on. |
| Chorus | 56 to 76 | The shadow screen. The two souls dance as wayang figures on rods. She goes away along the screen. |
| Bridge | 77 to 89 | His lives and her lives cross the stage. The kayon is planted. |
| Final chorus | 89 to 115 | Dawn at the same temple, now old. He arrives. She stops at the relief and touches her own face. She turns. He holds out his hand. She takes it. |
| Outro | 115 to 121 | The two stand together under the first sun. |
