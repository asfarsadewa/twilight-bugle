STYLE: A grand hand-made toy theatre production built from painted corrugated cardboard. Every piece is a flat cutout of cardboard about 4 mm thick, cut by hand with a craft knife. The front of each piece is painted in opaque matte gouache with visible brush strokes, dry-brush texture and a few touches of real gold leaf. Shapes are bold and readable from the back row. Details are painted by hand, never printed. Figures have elegant, slightly long storybook proportions, simple painted faces with dark almond eyes, soft rosy cheeks and a few confident brush lines. Puppet joints are brass split pins. Palette: night indigo #1B2340, deep slate #34425E, snow white #EDE7DA, stone grey #8C8F99, sun gold #E2A93B, ember red #B8452B, moon silver-blue #AFC3E3, dawn rose #E9A7A0, with raw cardboard brown #A57E52 at cut edges. The mood is grand, sentimental and warm, like a beloved fairy tale staged with love. Medieval fantasy that belongs to no real culture.
CONSTRAINTS: No text, no lettering, no numbers, no logos, no signature, no watermark. No photographic people, no 3D render look, no plastic, no glossy finish.
SUBJECT: Key art for the production. Inside a grand old toy theatre, the stage shows the walled city of Ilvara at night under the Long Night: a deep indigo painted sky with painted stars and no sun. Layered cardboard flats make the city: pale stone walls and towers with blue slate roofs and warm lit windows, and at the center the tall slender Lantern Spire with a glowing silver-blue lamp at its top. In front, a cardboard rampart. On the rampart stands Princess Ilune as a jointed cardboard rod puppet, about a third of the stage height: long silver-white hair, a silver crescent-moon tiara, a deep blue gown with silver embroidery, a moon-white cloak, holding up the Moonlamp, a silver lantern with a pale blue-white light. A thin wooden rod rises from a slot in the stage floor to her back. On the left, far away on a painted snowy hill, a tiny gold beacon fire glows. A painted crescent moon hangs on a visible wire.
COMPOSITION: Seen from the middle of the audience, straight on. The gilded proscenium arch and the deep crimson velvet house curtains, drawn open, frame the stage. A row of warm footlights along the front edge of the stage floor. Real theatre light: a warm follow spot on the princess, a cool blue wash on the city, soft real shadows of the flats on the flats behind them. The raw corrugated cardboard edges of the flats catch the light.
