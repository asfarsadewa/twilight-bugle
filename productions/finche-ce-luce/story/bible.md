**Finché c'è luce · As Long as There Is Light · Selama Masih Ada Cahaya · 只要还有光**

A fishing village on a rocky southern coast. Every winter the sea takes a boat. Matteo, Lucia and the village build a lighthouse stone by stone. Its beam brings the boats home for twenty years. Their son Nino grows up and goes to sea with the fleet. On the night of the great storm the clockwork of the lamp breaks. Matteo turns the lens by hand all night, and Lucia carries the oil up the stairs. The beam brings every boat home except Nino's. At dawn the sea brings back his boat, empty. That evening Lucia says: "Light it, Matteo. It is evening." They climb the stairs again.

## Against the production brief

- **What is lost:** Nino. His boat comes back empty. Nothing returns him, and the song never pretends otherwise.
- **What they must still do:** light the lamp every night for the sons of other mothers.
- **Where the light comes from:** their resolve. The light is the act of turning the wheel and climbing the stairs, not a reward.
- **The music:** minor key in every section. The storm is the loudest moment and the final chorus the biggest, and both stay grieving.

## Cast

| Figure | Part | Rig |
|---|---|---|
| Matteo | The builder and keeper. Baritone. | Articulated marionette: he lays stone, hauls rope and turns the wheel. |
| Lucia | His wife. Mezzo-soprano. | Articulated marionette: she carries water and oil, climbs the stairs and kneels. |
| Nino | Their son, as a baby in a rope basket, a boy and a young fisherman. | Basket on a string, boy card on a slide, young-man marionette |
| The village | Haulers, the fleet's women with lanterns, a choir on the quay | Rows on rods and slides that sway with the work |

## Scenes: everything is built, sailed and turned on stage

| Section | Stage |
|---|---|
| Intro | Night and a black sea. Three rows of waves rock. A small boat pitches on its wire and slips under the far row. Women hold lanterns on the rock. One bell tolls. |
| Verse 1 | The building. The cliff rises from the trap. Scaffolding flies in. The village hauls a rope in rhythm, and a stone block swings up on a pulley. The tower rises in four drums from the trap, one per line, as Matteo sets the stones. |
| Verse 2 | Lucia carries water and bread up the scaffold. The baby's rope basket hangs from a beam and rocks. Matteo lights the first flame in the lantern room as it flies down onto the tower. |
| Pre-chorus | The lens turns for the first time. The theatre's real beam sweeps through the haze across the stage and the house. The village on the shore kneels. |
| Chorus 1 | The boats come home one by one along the wave rows. The women run along the quay and lift their lanterns. Matteo turns the lens. |
| Verse 3 | Twenty years pass: the sun and the moon cross on their wires. The boy card grows into the young-man marionette. The fleet sails out at dawn, and Lucia counts the sails on her fingers. |
| Verse 4 | Matteo winds the clockwork. The iron weight sinks slowly down the tower on its chain. |
| Bridge | The storm. Rain, lightning, the three wave rows rocking hard, the boats pitching. The chain snaps, and the weight drops through the trap. Matteo throws his weight on the wheel. Lucia climbs the cutaway stair with the oil can. Sail after sail comes in on the beam. One mooring stays empty. |
| Break | Grey dawn. The rain stops. An empty boat slides slowly onto the sand. Lucia kneels and puts her hand on its side. |
| Final chorus | Night again. The women line the quay with lanterns for other boats. Lucia climbs the stairs. Matteo turns the wheel. The beam sweeps the sea, then the whole house. |
| Outro | The cello. Lucia's whisper. The lamp is lit again. The traveller closes, and the beam goes on turning behind it. |
