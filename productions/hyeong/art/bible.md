# Art bible: 형 먼저 가

Version 2. Every image request for this production starts with the locked text below. A change to the locked text makes version 2.

The sky never comes from a painting. The theatre's cyclorama lights it. The rain and the fog come from the stage.

The style is charcoal and ink wash with thin watercolour on Korean mulberry paper, like a painter's field sketchbook of 1950. The colour is drained by the rain. The paper and one dull red carry it.

The imagery stays with the people of the road, and it shows no violence. As in the film, the two sides are told apart only by their clothes: olive drab with steel helmets, or quilted mustard khaki with ear-flap caps. Each line carries one plain banner of unmarked cloth, blue or red. No insignia, no emblems, no armbands and no badges. No piece shows a real person.

Version 2 allows the plain banners. The pieces painted under version 1 do not change.

STYLE: A grand hand-made toy theatre production built from printed card. Every piece is a flat cutout of thick card about 4 mm thick, cut by hand with a craft knife. The front of each piece is painted like a page from a Korean painter's field sketchbook of 1950: soft charcoal line and grey ink wash with thin, rain-washed watercolour on warm hanji mulberry paper, with the long fibres of the paper visible, loose expressive brush and charcoal strokes, quiet tender faces drawn with a few lines. The colour is muted and wet: slate grey, mist white, earth brown, faded indigo, pine green, with one dull persimmon red for a few accents. Bold, simple shapes that read from the back row. Puppet joints are brass split pins. Palette: hanji paper #EDE6D6, charcoal #2B2A28, slate #5E6670, mist #C9CDD0, earth #6E5A44, indigo #3B4A66, pine #4E5E48, persimmon #B4553A, with raw card brown #A57E52 at cut edges. The mood is grief, tenderness and rain. Korea, June 1950.
CONSTRAINTS: No text, no characters, no letters, no numbers, no Hangul, no signs with writing, no logos, no signature, no watermark. No national flags, no insignia, no emblems, no armbands, no badges, no stars on caps. A banner is plain unmarked cloth of one colour. No weapons in use, no violence. No real persons. No photographic people, no 3D render look, no plastic, no glossy finish, no anime style.
