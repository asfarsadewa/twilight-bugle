# 형은 여기 있다 · Your Brother Is Here

A companion song to *형 먼저 가*, told entirely by Cheol, the older brother who crossed the bridge.

For this companion, Min and Cheol are blood brothers, as requested by the user. This revises the earlier description of them as village friends. Their shared mother, the rice ball, the June rain, the bridge and the promise of home belong to both songs.

War caused the separation. Refugees fled across the river while soldiers marched against their flow. Chol crossed; Min stayed to hold the crossing as opposing troops closed in. Min severed the bridge. Different uniforms and plain unmarked banners stood behind the brothers on the two banks. The gap was enforced by war, and it did not become freely crossable when the fighting stopped.

Many years have passed. Chol's hair is white. At dusk he stands on the relatively developed bank, beside a paved embankment, steel railing, streetlight and closed access gate. Apartment blocks glow behind his side. Across the river are sparse worn farmhouses, fields, a dirt track, utility poles and a few dim windows. Neither side is named. He looks towards the place where they grew up, wondering whether Min may still be there. In memory Min is still nineteen, giving him his own share of food and telling him to go first, to stay alive and go home.

Cheol has lived because his little brother asked him to. He also carries the shame of being the older one who was supposed to protect the younger. He wonders whether Min cried after he turned away. Perhaps Min is watching the same sunset. Perhaps he too calls his brother's name at dusk.

The first refrain asks whether Min is still there. The final refrain answers the original song's departure: this time the older brother will stay and wait. The plain domestic call, "It gets cold after sunset; let us go home together," is addressed across a river that gives no answer.

Min's fate remains unknown. There is no actual reunion, no reply from Min and no confirmation that Cheol's view or memory is shared by him. The song ends with Cheol saying, "Min, your brother is here."

## Motifs

- Dusk now, June monsoon rain then.
- Refugees escaping against the flow of marching troops; two opposing lines and the severed crossing.
- The same river and banks through war and a materially unequal peace. Development has changed the view without healing the family.
- A closed gate where the older man can look across, but cannot simply walk to his brother.
- Cheol's white hair, Min forever nineteen in his memory.
- Cooking smoke, a mother's rice ball and a home that cannot feel like home without the missing brother.
- The older brother's promise to protect, fulfilled instead by the younger.
- "Go first" becomes "This time I will stay."

## Musical direction

A majestic, deeply melodic Korean orchestral ballad. One mature male lead, warm and low in the verses, rising into a ringing, sustained upper register in the choruses. The emotion comes from the melody, the words and the orchestral swell. A huge final chorus opens back into a solitary ending, without resolving the separation.

The user approved the final Suno recording, delivered under the title *형은 여기 있다*. Its duration is 4:02.800. The original working title was *아직 거기 있니*. The final staging and exact section plan are in `../production-notes.md`.

## Continuity sources

The original Hyeong theatre production and the sibling `mini-mv-studio/mvs/hyeong/brief.json`, `film.py`, `plates/generate.py` and `review/notes.json` establish the military cause. In the original review at 113.02 seconds, the user specifically required military presence and marching soldiers for the emotional delivery. That requirement governs this companion too. The user's later blood-brother decision overrides the earliest brief's village-friend description.

The new show views the river from the opposite direction to the original theatre's climactic tableau: Chol is LEFT on the escape bank, Min RIGHT on the bank where he remained. This geography never reverses within the companion. Min only appears in the cold memories. No present-day figure confirms his fate. The spellings Cheol in source files and Chol in the programme refer to the same older brother.
