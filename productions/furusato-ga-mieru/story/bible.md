# Story bible: ふるさとが見える

Show XIV, pitched and approved on 19 November 2026. `../song/suno-style.txt`, `../song/suno-exclude.txt` and `../song/suno-lyrics.txt` are the Suno handoff. The producer supplied the recording on the same day.

**ふるさとが見える · Furusato ga Mieru · I Can See Home · Kampung Halaman Terlihat · 看得见故乡**

Japan, the Meiji era. Every spring the girls of a poor mountain village walk over a snowy pass to the silk mills in the valley. They reel silk from boiling cocoons fourteen hours a day and send their wages home. Hana goes at twelve. By sixteen she is the best reeler on the floor. Then the cough comes, and blood on the white thread. The mill strikes her name off and sends word to her family. Her brother Kichi runs over the pass in the blizzard and carries her home on his back. At dawn, on the top of the pass, she sees their valley below. She says "Ah, I can see home," and she dies there. He carries her down. In spring the youngest sister, Yuki, ties her straw sandals and walks over the same pass to the same mill, because the family must eat. Kichi walks her to the top.

The story draws on the history of the girls who crossed the mountain passes to the silk mills of central Japan. The names, the village and the mill are invented. No piece shows a real person or a real company.

## Against the production brief

- **What is lost:** Hana. She dies on the pass with home in sight. Nothing returns her.
- **What they must still do:** send the next girl. Yuki crosses the pass in spring, and Kichi walks her to the top.
- **Where the light comes from:** what they do for each other. Hana sends her wages. Kichi carries her through the night. He ties his little sister's sandals. None of it saves anyone.
- **The music:** minor and pentatonic in every section. The mill song drives, the blizzard is the loudest part, and the last chorus is the biggest. All of it stays grieving.

## Cast

| Figure | Part | Rig |
|---|---|---|
| Hana | The reeler, from twelve to sixteen. Lead voice. | Marionette: she reels, bows, coughs, falls and is carried |
| Kichi | Her elder brother. Second voice. | Marionette in a straw rain cape and a bamboo hat |
| Kichi carrying Hana | The two as one figure on the pass | A jointed pair: his head and arms move, and her head rests on his shoulder |
| Yuki | The youngest sister, twelve, in the last scene | Card on a slide |
| The mill girls | The reeling floor and the line on the pass | Rows on rods and slides that work in rhythm |
| Mother | At the lamp in the farmhouse | Card on a rod |

## Scenes: everything is worked on stage

| Section | Stage |
|---|---|
| Intro | Wind and snow on the pass. A line of small girls, roped together, slides up the mountain. A lamp burns in a farmhouse in the valley. |
| Verse 1 | Spring at twelve. Hana ties her sandals at the farmhouse door. Mother gives her a rice ball. The line of girls climbs the pass, and Hana looks back. |
| Verse 2 | The mill flies in: tall reeling frames with wheels and a long belt. Steam rises from the basins. The girls reel in rhythm, and the wheels turn. Hana writes her letter by lamplight. |
| Pre-chorus | The mill song: "引け 引け 白い糸". Every reel turns on the beat, and the overseer's bell rings. |
| Chorus 1 | The mill fades to Hana's dream. The pass, and beyond it the farmhouse lamp. She reaches for it. |
| Verse 3 | The cough. A red thread runs off her reel. The mill gate closes. She is carried out on a door board. |
| Verse 4 | Kichi runs over the pass in the blizzard. He kneels, lifts her and takes her on his back. |
| Bridge | The climb. Snow, wind and a dark sky. He climbs step by step, slips and gets up. The choir calls "登れ 登れ". The sky turns white in the east. |
| Break | The top of the pass at dawn. "兄さん 降ろして." He sets her down. The valley and the farmhouse lamp come up below. "ああ…見える." |
| Final chorus | The sun rises over the valley. She is still. He sits beside her in the snow. Then he lifts her again and carries her down towards the lamp. |
| Outro | Spring. The snow on the pass is still white. Yuki ties her sandals at the door. Kichi walks her up the pass. At the top he lets go of her hand, and she goes on down to the mill with the other girls. He watches. |
