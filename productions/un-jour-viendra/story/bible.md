# Un jour viendra

终有一天 · Suatu Hari Nanti · A Day Will Come

A village boy joins the imperial army as its lowest servant. He sweeps the courtyards and carries the water. His spirit never goes out. The song is in French. Shan sings it.

## Premise

Shan is eighteen. He grew up in Cloud Brook, a village of rice terraces in the misty mountains.

The emperor calls the young men to the army. Shan leaves at dawn. Mei, the girl from the tea terraces, gives him a sprig of plum blossom. The plum blooms in the cold, when no other flower dares.

At the great camp below the wall, Shan is the lowest of all. He sweeps the courtyards before the sun. He carries water and washes the armour of other men. The soldiers laugh at the mountain boy.

Shan says nothing. At night he practises with his broom as if it were a spear. He talks to the moon, which shines on Mei too. He dreams of the day he carries the banner and leads a real army for the pride of his village.

The song ends in the present. Shan is still the boy with the broom. His spirit burns brighter than any lamp in the camp.

## The world

Mythical China, seen as an ink painting. Mountains rise out of the mist. Pines cling to the cliffs. A long wall runs along the ridges.

A dragon of cloud and ink lives in the sky. It watches the brave. A crane carries messages between the mountains.

## Motifs

**The plum blossom.** It blooms in the cold. It is Mei's gift and Shan's courage.

**The broom.** His only weapon. In his dreams it becomes a spear and a banner pole.

**The moon.** The same moon shines on the camp and on the village.

## Art direction

A beautiful, solemn Chinese ink painting. Wet and dry ink on rice paper, with mist and empty space. One red seal and the plum blossoms are the only strong colours. Gold leaf touches the dragon and the banners.
