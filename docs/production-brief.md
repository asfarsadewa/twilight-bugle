# The Twilight Bugle: standing production brief

User direction, 16 November 2026. Applies to every new show and every work-in-progress revision from this point forward.

**The Twilight Bugle is a tragedy theatre.**

1. **Every show belongs to tragedy.** Its losses and consequences must be real and remain real at the end.
2. **There is no happy ending.** Suffering does not buy a compensating reward, fulfilled dream or consoling reversal.
3. **Any happiness comes from the characters' will to move forward.** Light lives in their resolve, care and continued action, rather than in obtaining happiness. Tenderness belongs in what they do for each other; it does not cancel the tragedy.
4. **One's gotta do what one's gotta do.** Characters act because something must be done, even when doing it cannot save what they love or earn them a better fate.

**The music is always on the sad side, in every show and every section.** Visual satire, physical comedy and moments of happiness may exist inside the tragedy. The score must retain sorrow beneath them. A comic image or an affectionate gesture is not a reason for the music to become jaunty, festive or triumphant.

The emotional climax is the cost of continuing and the decision to continue. A swelling orchestra, a dance or a shared gesture must preserve that cost. Musical catharsis must not turn into victory or celebration. A brief moment of happiness within the tragedy does not turn the ending into a happy one or erase the central loss.

The puppets remain the performers. Necessity drives their movement: reaching, carrying, pulling, balancing, falling, recovering, helping and taking the next step. Grief and a strong physical pulse can coexist. Dance can be a plea, an obligation, an attempt to hold something together or a farewell.

Before handing off any pitch or song prompt, assess the final scene: what has been irretrievably lost, what the characters must still do, and where any light comes from. If the ending rewards their suffering with achieved happiness, revise it.
